Who it is for

Built around one person and one question

“Should I buy this land, and how much money do I need to survive until collections catch up?”

A small or mid-size developer in Mumbai or Pune: knows construction and the local market, is not a finance modeller, and has to defend the numbers to a lender or investor. Today the decision rests on a spreadsheet few people trust.

Composite persona from desk research, to be confirmed in interviews.

What he needs, in order

  1. A trustworthy go/no-go, fast
  2. Peak funding, the quarter it hits, and the equity/loan split
  3. “What if” without rebuilding the model
  4. Numbers a lender will accept, including a downside case
  5. The most he can pay for the land
  6. A source and date for every input
How it works

Eight steps from land price to verdict

The project is modelled quarter by quarter, from the land purchase to the last buyer payment. Every step is a small, tested calculation you can trace on screen.

  1. 1

    Areas

    Plot × FSI gives built-up area; a saleable ratio gives the area you sell.

  2. 2

    Costs

    Land and stamp duty up front, approvals and premiums before construction, escalating construction cost along an S-curve.

  3. 3

    Sales

    A share of the saleable area sells each quarter at a price that grows each year.

  4. 4

    Collections

    Buyers pay a booking amount, then in step with construction, then the rest at possession.

  5. 5

    RERA account

    A set share of collections is locked and released only as construction is completed.

  6. 6

    Funding

    Equity covers shortfalls first, then the loan. The loan is repaid from free cash only.

  7. 7

    Results

    NPV, IRR, payback, profit on cost, peak funding and lender ratios.

  8. 8

    Break-evens

    The sale price and the land price at which the project exactly meets your hurdle.

Indian rules built in

Not a generic spreadsheet

RERA s.4(2)(l)(D)

The separate RERA account

70% of collections by default (an input, since states can vary it), drawn only for land and construction in step with completion.

RERA s.13

Booking cap

A warning if the booking amount is above 10% before a registered agreement.

RERA s.18

Delay interest

A late project owes interest to buyers; the delay scenario counts it.

Premiums

Their own cost line

FSI, fungible and TDR premiums are shown separately, because in Mumbai they are a large cost.

GST

Passed through

GST collected from buyers goes to the government, so revenue is shown net of GST.

Income tax

Rate as an input

Charged on cumulative profit with losses carried forward; confirm the rate against the Act.

Where the market inputs come from

  • Sale price and growth: Knight Frank, India Real Estate H1 2026 (Pune average ₹10,063/sq ft, +5% a year)
  • Construction cost and escalation: ANAROCK, 2021–2025 (₹3,604/sq ft in 2025; 7.7% a year computed from its figures)
  • Land value floor: IGR Maharashtra ready reckoner rates (eASR)
  • Rules: RERA Act 2016, Sections 4, 13 and 18

Every input in the tool shows its source and date, or is flagged as an assumption or an example placeholder. A blank or a labelled guess is better than an invented number.

Limits and privacy

What it does not do

Limits

  • One project, one phase. Joint-development and revenue-share deals are not modelled yet, although they are common in Indian cities.
  • Simplified tax timing, and the RERA account balance is released at possession.
  • The verdict thresholds (hurdle rate, minimum profit on cost) are assumptions to set for your own business.
  • The example project is illustrative. It shows how the tool works, not an investment answer.

Your data

  • Inputs are sent to a calculation function, used once, and not stored or logged by the tool.
  • Your inputs are saved only in your own browser, so you can come back to them.
  • The investment memo is written by a fixed template. No AI service sees your deal.
  • For a confidential deal, run the open-source version on your own computer.
